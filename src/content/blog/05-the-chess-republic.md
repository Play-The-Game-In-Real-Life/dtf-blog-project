The Chess Republic was the most intellectually stimulating realm in the archive, and I will be taking no questions about whether that was a good thing.

## The constitution

Everything in the Republic was a match. Where to eat was a match. Whose turn it was to text was a match. Even whether to stay in on a Friday became a series of opening moves, sacrifices, and a long middle game involving a concession I never fully received.

It was extremely fun, right up until it wasn't.

## The great war of who texts last

The decisive conflict was a ten-day cold war about who texted last. Neither of us wanted to be the one to reach out, because reaching out meant losing, and we were both far too good at the game to lose.

The result was a perfect stalemate, played flawlessly, with total strategic discipline, by two people who missed each other the entire time.

## The part I would like to dispute

For the record, I won most of the arguments. I would like that on the record. I also lost the Republic, which is the kind of outcome you only get when you optimize for the wrong win condition.

## The lesson, which took years

Winning an argument is a losing strategy if the person you are arguing with is the person you wanted to be with. I understand this now. I understood it in the moment, too, but I was busy being right, which is a full-time job with no benefits.

## Current relations

Cold peace. Occasional likes. We have both chosen to remain at the board, but neither of us has touched a piece in a long time.

*Verdict: a very good opponent. A poor reason to lose a person.*
