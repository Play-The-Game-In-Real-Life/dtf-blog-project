The Long Distance Provinces were less a kingdom than a very well-maintained network. Two capitals, three time zones' worth of trouble, and one connection that held up beautifully as long as nobody tried to hug anybody.

## The infrastructure

This is the one realm I can discuss in my native tongue. We had scheduled calls. We had a shared calendar with color-coding. We had a protocol for the flight-booking window, and I tracked ticket prices like I was monitoring a production cluster.

On paper it was a masterpiece. Uptime was 99.9%. Nothing ever dropped. We never missed a call.

## The bug

The thing about uptime is that it measures whether the line is open, not whether anyone is home. For long stretches I was on the call and not *there*, saying "I'm fine" in a rotating set of tones, each tuned to a different level of fine. Belle, who was extremely good at hearing the difference, kept asking which version I meant.

I had no answer, because I did not know. I only knew how to keep the connection alive.

## The fall

The province dissolved the way those things do: slowly, then on a Tuesday. The last conversation was kind, which made it worse. Neither of us could afford the route, and neither of us had the nerve to say that the route was not really the problem.

## What I learned

You cannot automate presence. I tried, and the monitoring dashboard was excellent. It was completely useless the one time somebody needed me to put the dashboard down.

*Status: dissolved. The time zones remain. I still know what time it is there, and I try not to use that information.*
