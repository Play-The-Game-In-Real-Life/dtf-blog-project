Some kingdoms have a founding date. The Free City of Almost has a vibe and a disputed border.

## The charter

It was never declared a city, which means it could never technically be dissolved. Everyone involved agreed that it was something. Nobody could agree on what, and nobody wanted to be the first to file the paperwork.

We had all the amenities of a real realm: inside jokes, a standing weekly thing, a toothbrush at one address and a hoodie at another. We just never had a word for any of it.

## The legal situation

Under Almost law, neither party was required to define anything. Neither party was allowed to ask. The only question we permitted ourselves was "so what are you up to later?", which carried the entire diplomatic weight of the city and held, bravely, for months.

## The administrative backlog

I treated it like a ticket nobody triaged. It was opened, it was acknowledged, and it sat in the queue marked *pending*, assigned to no one, with a label that said "we'll circle back." I have worked on systems with fewer unresolved items than this relationship.

## The end that was not one

There was no collapse. The replies just got further apart, the way a signal fades when you drive out of range. By the time either of us noticed, the city had been quiet for a while, and there was nobody to declare it over.

## Lessons

Ask the question. I know. I have written this sentence in other chronicles. I will write it in future ones.

*Status: never officially founded. Borders still disputed. Fantome, if you read this: I am sorry about the toothbrush. Keep it. It is a gift now.*
