Here is a list of things in my life that are fully redundant, monitored, and automatically backed up:

- My files
- My photos
- My server configuration
- A small AI tool that watches the fleet and tells me when a machine goes quiet

And here is a list of things I have an equivalent system for:

- *(intentionally left blank)*

## The pattern

Whenever a relationship ends, I respond the same way. I do not call a friend. I do not journal. I go to the machines. Within a week I have a new service running, a cleaner network, and some kind of uptime graph I did not need.

I once rebuilt an entire node, from scratch, in a single night, rather than admit that a text message had hurt my feelings. The node came out great.

## Why infrastructure feels safe

Servers do what you tell them to. They tell you exactly what is wrong in a log file, they come back after a restart, and a failure has a fix. You can recreate a machine from a config. You cannot, it turns out, recreate a person from a config, and I have tried to find the flag for that.

## The part where I admit something

The reason I keep building is that building is the one form of care I trust myself to do correctly. I would love to extend that to other areas.

## The actual roadmap

1. Take one of those evenings, the ones I would normally spend on the fleet, and call someone.
2. Say what is wrong, out loud, in sentences.
3. Do not set up monitoring for the conversation.

Step three is the hard one.
