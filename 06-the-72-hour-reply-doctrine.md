This is a documented pattern in the Capital, observed over several years by a source with unlimited access and zero objectivity (me).

## Phase one: the read

A message arrives. I read it immediately, in full, within seconds. I understand it. I know exactly what it asks, and I even know what I would like to say.

## Phase two: the draft

I then compose a response. It is thoughtful, warm, and perfectly calibrated. It acknowledges the emotional subtext. It is four paragraphs long. I read it back and conclude it is too much.

I delete it.

## Phase three: the silence

I write a shorter version, find it too cold, and leave it unsent. At this point I tell myself I will reply later, when I have the right words. This is the beginning of the 72-hour window, a period during which the message sits in my head, getting heavier, while the actual person waits.

## Phase four: the burst

On day three, something finally cracks. I send a reply. Then another, because I forgot something. Then a joke to make up for the delay. Then a clarification of the joke. By the end I have sent fourteen messages, which is more than the entire first two years of some relationships.

## Phase five: the "haha yeah"

If the situation is actually serious, I will skip all of the above and send "haha yeah." I do not recommend this, and I am the leading practitioner.

## Reforms under consideration

- Replying within a time frame measured in hours.
- Sending one message instead of fourteen.
- Sending the first draft, which is almost always fine.

All three have been under review since roughly the Second Age.
