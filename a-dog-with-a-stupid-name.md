We argued about the name for three weeks and landed on something so ridiculous that neither of us could say it to a vet with a straight face.

He was not a smart dog. He was a good dog, which is better. He waited at the window at exactly the same hour every day, and no one ever taught him to, and I never found out what he was waiting for.

He slept in the worst possible spot, at the foot of the stairs, so that every midnight trip to the kitchen became a small negotiation. *Excuse me. Sorry. I need water.* He would sigh and move one inch.

In this version, I am better at things. I wake up when I am supposed to. I take the long way home through the park because he likes it. I learn the names of the neighbors, because he does, and he introduces me.

You laugh at me in the rain with a leash in one hand and a bag in the other. I am carrying the umbrella, and there is a dog between us, and neither of us minds that it is raining.

It is a very small life. I would have taken it.
