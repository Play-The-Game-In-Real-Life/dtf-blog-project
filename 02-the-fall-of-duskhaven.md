Duskhaven was the first realm, and for a long time it was the only one. It had a good couch, a shared streaming account, and a summer that lasted what felt like three years and was, in fact, three years.

## The golden age

In its prime, Duskhaven ran on small rituals. Someone always knew what the other one wanted for dinner. There was a drawer. You know the one: the drawer that quietly becomes shared, with one set of chargers and two people's worth of receipts. I thought we had built something permanent.

I was wrong about that, but I was right about the couch.

## The decline

Nobody sacked the city. There was no siege, no villain, no dramatic betrayal at the gates. Duskhaven fell the way old infrastructure falls: nobody did the upkeep. A small crack went unpatched, then another, until one day the whole thing was held together by assumptions.

I want to be clear that I saw it happening. I read every strained conversation like a changelog, noting the exact commit where things got worse, and then I did nothing with the information. I am a person who monitors a system extremely closely and fixes nothing. This is, it turns out, a pattern.

## The aftermath

The ruins of Duskhaven are still standing, and I visit them at night, in the form of one specific playlist. I am told this is not a recovery strategy.

## Verdict

It was a good kingdom, and it deserved a better steward. Duskhaven did not fall because it was weak. It fell because the person in charge thought paying attention was the same as taking care.
